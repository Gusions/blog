Planning a trip means scattered pieces: tickets in your email, costs in a spreadsheet, a list of restaurants in a note, the itinerary in your head. Atlas keeps each trip as a folder of notes in your vault and puts the pieces together in one place, from the first idea to the last receipt.

## One trip, six views

- **Overview.** Dates, destinations and the numbers that matter at a glance: what has been spent, what is still to pay, who owes whom.
- **Itinerary.** The trip day by day, stop by stop.
- **Bookings.** Flights, trains and stays, with their details in one list.
- **Costs.** Every expense with its category, who paid and whether it counts towards the shared total.
- **Documents.** Tickets, reservations and other files, attached to the trip.
- **Places.** Restaurants, bars and sights, each as its own note, shown on a map.

## Shared costs that add up

Travelling with other people means splitting expenses. Atlas tracks who paid for what and works out a clear balance of who owes whom. Only expenses that have actually been paid count towards the balance; anything still to pay is shown separately, so the numbers never lie.

Costs can stay in sync with a Google Sheet, in both directions. Adding an expense in Obsidian writes it to the sheet, and people who don't use Obsidian can keep adding expenses there. The columns of the sheet and the fields in Atlas are the same, so nothing gets lost in translation.

## Places on a map

Each place is a note with its city, type and coordinates. Atlas can look up the position of places that don't have one yet, checking that the result matches the name and is near the city before saving it. The map uses Obsidian's own Maps view.

## How it works

A trip is a folder with a few plain notes: overview, itinerary, logistics, budget and attachments. You can read and edit them like any other note; Atlas is the view on top. It works on desktop and mobile, with a quiet, text-first design and a coloured cover for each trip.

Requires Obsidian 1.10 or later.
