A wardrobe is a database nobody queries.

Mine lives in [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md), because everything I care about lives there. One note per garment, a photo, a few fields: type, colour, brand, season. It is tidy. It is also silent. A note can tell me I own a jacket. It can't show me how it looks next to the trousers I'm about to put on.

What I want from a wardrobe is simple to say and annoying to do. I don't want to dress the same way every day. I don't want to build every outfit around one favourite piece. I want to see everything I own at once, and be shown combinations I wouldn't have tried on my own.

I don't need an app to tell me not to wear two sweatshirts. What I need is a prompt: the weather, a piece I want to wear, and a few suggestions around it. The rules only exist so the suggestions don't look absurd. Shorts are for summer. Home clothes are not outfits. A layer is a layer. None of it is deep, but it had never been written anywhere.

Photos are what a list of notes is bad at, and suggestions are what a folder can't give. So I built a small plugin, [[your-contacts-are-just-notes|like the one for my contacts]], that reads the notes where they are and puts the clothes in front of me. I called it Rack.

What I like is that the notes stay mine, in plain files, and the tool can disappear tomorrow without taking anything with it. It only helps me look at what I already own and decide.

Own the notes. Then give them a window.
