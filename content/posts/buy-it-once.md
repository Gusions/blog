title: Buy it once

description: Why I choose clothes that get better with time.
---

A jacket should get better the longer you wear it. Most don't.

Most clothes are at their best on the day you buy them. The fabric is stiff, the color is right, the seams are flat. Then the washing starts. You wear it for a season, then you replace it.

Work clothes are built on the opposite idea. Carhartt started in Michigan in 1889, making overalls for railroad workers. Those men needed clothes that survived the job, so the clothes were made to survive it.

The first thing you notice is the canvas. It's thick and a little rough, and it softens in its own time, fading where you bend and carry. I like clothes that show time instead of hiding it.

I had mine for years. I lost it, and I miss it.

![Carhartt jacket](/assets/carhartt-jacket.jpg)
*The kind of jacket I mean.*

There's also trust. After a few seasons you stop wondering whether it will hold. You stop thinking about it at all, and that's the point.

Then there's repair, the part nobody advertises. When a good jacket tears, you patch it. A patch on workwear is a record, not a flaw.

The honest limits: it's heavy. It takes weeks to break in. It's wrong for a hot day and wrong for a formal room. If you want clothes that feel perfect on day one, this isn't the brand.

Choosing one brand isn't loyalty. It's a way to stop deciding. One less thing to compare, one less thing to replace.

Buy it once. Wear it until it's yours.
