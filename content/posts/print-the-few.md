---
title: Print the few you love
date: 16/11/2026
topics: [thoughts]
description: A camera roll keeps every photo. A wall keeps the ones that matter.
---

Your phone holds thousands of photos. You almost never look at them.

They arrive without effort. Every dinner, every sunset, every screenshot you meant to delete. The phone sorts them by date and backs them up while you sleep. Nothing is lost, and nothing is seen.

A print on the wall is different. Someone chose it from a hundred shots that looked almost the same. Someone paid for the paper, the frame and a nail in the plaster. You walk past it every morning, and every morning you see it a little differently, because you are a little different.

Friendship works the same way.

Social networks are the camera roll of people. Everyone you have ever met is in there, in perfect order. Old classmates, a colleague from three jobs ago, someone you sat next to at a wedding. You scroll past faces the way you scroll past photos of a dinner you don't remember. Once in a while the app brings one back to you. You smile, and keep scrolling.

The roll counts how many. The wall shows how much.

Good friends are prints, not files. You don't know them as thumbnails. You know them full size, in the light of the room they hang in. You know where the colour has faded and where the frame is chipped, and you like them more for it. A file never fades. That is why nobody looks at it.

A print also asks for care. Light changes it. Dust settles on the glass. Now and then you take it down, clean it, and hang it straight again. Friendship asks for the same small maintenance: a call when nothing is wrong, a visit nobody planned.

And a wall has limits. Every frame takes space you could give to something else, so you choose. Some prints come down after years, and the wall keeps a paler square where they hung. The mark stays. So does what it meant.

A wall doesn't grow by adding. It grows by looking. You can't hang everything and still see it.

Abundance teaches you to scroll. Scarcity teaches you to look.

Print the few. Hang them where you live.
