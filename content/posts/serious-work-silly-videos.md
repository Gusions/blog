---
title: Serious work, silly videos
date: 09/12/2025
topics: [thoughts]
description: Serious businesses keep making silly videos. Attention is not trust.
---

Someone walks toward the camera. They stop. They point at a caption floating in the air. A beat, a smirk, a cut.

This is a bakery. This is a garage. This is a company that installs boilers, fits kitchens, cleans offices, ships pallets. Real work, real margins, real customers with real problems.

We have a new genre, and its one rule is motion without meaning. Walk in. Walk out. Point up. Point down. Lip-sync a trending sound over an invoice. Dance next to a van. Open a door, shrug, close it. The product never appears. The owner always does.

Then come the sketches. The boss plays the grumpy customer. The intern plays the boss. The mechanic rolls his eyes at a "stupid question" nobody asked. Everyone plays someone, and nobody plays themselves.

I understand the logic. Attention is scarce, and a face is cheap. Someone told you to post every day, and a walk toward the lens takes ten seconds. But attention is not trust, and a clip is not a customer. A hundred thousand views will not fill the order book. They will not answer the question a buyer asks at nine at night: can I rely on these people?

That question has a short list of answers. How you work. What it costs. What happens when something goes wrong. What a finished job looks like. Every one of them is more interesting than a smirk, and every one of them is already in your business. You live inside it. You just don't film it.

Show the dough at five in the morning. Show the fault code, the part, the repair, the bill. Show the quote broken down line by line. Show the job you got wrong and how you fixed it. Proof beats performance. A plain video of a real task, with a real voice explaining it, will do more for a small company than a month of choreography.

Amateur is not the problem. Amateur can be the best thing on the feed: a shaky phone, a noisy workshop, an owner who knows his craft and says so. The problem is amateur acting, a personality performed to sell a service that never asked for one.

And there is a cost nobody counts. Every sketch teaches the customer what you are. If you are a joke, you are priced like a joke. If you are a trend, you are replaced by the next trend. The company that spends years earning trust can spend it in thirty seconds of pointing at the ceiling.

If you are good at what you do, say what you do. If you are not an actor, don't act. If the camera is on, give it something worth looking at.

Stop walking at the camera. Show the work.
